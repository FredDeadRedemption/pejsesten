# Thornwake (green)

Hero: **The Empress**, 30 hp. 25 cards, 11 distinct.

A midrange board deck built on windows. The bodies are deliberately a point under curve, so
on their own they trade badly and the deck looks harmless. The auras are what make them
real, and an aura lasts three turns. Thornwake does not win by having the better board, it
wins by having the better board *for three turns* and spending them well.

Pairs with [Tollgate](deck-tollgate.md), which is the same idea with the sign flipped.

## How it plays

Turns 1-3 are about width, not pressure. Briar Elf, Grovewarden, Thicket Nanny: every body
is cheap, most of them are bad, and a bramble costs nothing extra. Letting the opponent
trade up into you in this stretch is fine. What you are buying is a board of four or five
things for the turn the aura lands.

Turn 3 or 4 you open the window. Wildgrowth on a four-wide board is +4 +4 of stats in one
card, and nothing can kill it - there is no body to answer, only the counter ticking 3, 2,
1. From that moment both players are reading the same number and the whole game is the same
question asked twice: you decide what to spend three turns on, and the opponent decides
whether to fight you in them or sit under a Guard and wait.

Greenmantle is the other half, and it is the opposite trade: a killable aura that never
lapses. She is a Human carrying the banner, so she does not lift herself. A deck that drew only Greenmantles loses to removal; a deck that drew only
Wildgrowths wins three turns and then has a board of 1/2s again. Holding one of each is
when Thornwake is at its best.

Greenwake is the card that stops the window being pure tempo. Its Fanfare is the aura's own
effect pulled forward onto one body and left there permanently, so when the counter runs out
you still have a 4/5 Elf standing in the wreckage. Put it on something that survives.

Hold the Grove is the deck's only protection and it is entirely public. The opponent reads
*if you cast an incantation this turn, I draw two cards*, looks at the sweeper in their hand, and has
to decide. Playing it around costs them the turn they wanted; playing into it costs them the
card advantage they were buying. Thornwake wins either branch, which is the point - but only
if you lay the wager down *before* the board is worth sweeping, not after.

Thornwake has no removal at all. Every enemy minion dies in combat or not at all, and that is
the deck's defining weakness. It is also why the aura timing matters so much: the aura is the
removal.

## Keeps

Keep Briar Elf, Grovewarden, Thorn Dancer, Thicket Nanny. Keep Wildgrowth only with two
one- or two-drops already in hand - it is a blank on an empty board. Throw back Elder Oak,
Greenwake and Quickening always. The deck has 14 cards costing 1 or 2, so a three card hand
sees one about 90% of the time.

## Tuning notes

- Wildgrowth at 3 is the number least sure of itself. Zoo costs 1 for a permanent +1 +1 to
  your Beasts, which makes a three-turn version at 3 look expensive. The defence is that
  Wildgrowth also lifts everything played during the window, brambles included, and cannot
  be answered. If it keeps rotting in hand, drop it to 2.
- Eleven minions across the whole list and five of them brambles means the board cap of 7
  is reachable. Count before a Thicket Nanny.
- Greenwake's Fanfare is the pattern the set should hold to: an aura Fanfare is the aura's
  own effect pulled forward and left behind, never a second card stapled on. If an aura
  Fanfare starts doing something unrelated, the card is two cards.
- Thorn Dancer is the only Blitz and the only reason the deck can convert a window into
  damage on the turn it opens. Three copies is the floor, not the target.
- Greenwake's Fanfare is targeted, so the card is unplayable with no elf on the board, the
  same rule any targeted spell follows. It is a four drop that needs a board already.
- Two Elder Oaks is the whole top end on purpose. A third big body turns the deck into a
  curve-out, and the point of the deck is the three turns, not the six drop.

## Matchup: against Tollgate

Tollgate's auras subtract attack from your board and its wager punishes you for going to
the hero. The two windows cancel, so the real skill is sequencing yours into the gap in
theirs. Opening Wildgrowth into a live Hollowing is a wasted card - your 2/2s go to 3/3s and
back to 2/3s and you paid 3 for nothing.

Foreclosure is the card to play around. It steals your *rightmost* minion, so the order you
play things in is the counterplay: commit the junk body last and the omen takes a bramble.
