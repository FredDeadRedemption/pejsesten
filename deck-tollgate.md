# Tollgate (black)

Hero: **The Tower**, 30 hp. 25 cards, 12 distinct.

The same deck as [Thornwake](deck-thornwake.md) with the sign flipped. Where Thornwake adds
to its own board for three turns, Tollgate takes away from yours. Fewer bodies, tougher
ones, and a price on every line the opponent wants to take: a wager that taxes the swing,
an omen that taxes the commit, and auras that quietly make their whole board worse at
fighting.

It is not a control deck. It has one sweeper and no card draw engine, and from turn four it
is attacking.

## How it plays

Gatewatch at 1/4 is the whole early game. It does not trade and it is not meant to; it
stands in front of things for three turns while you reach the mana where your cards are
better than theirs. Take the chip damage. Tollgate's life total is a resource and spending
twelve of it before turn five is normal.

Hollowing is the hinge card. A board-wide -1 attack reads small and plays enormous, because
almost every cheap minion in the format has one or two attack and taking one off a 2/2
halves it. Fire it on the turn the opponent has committed, not on an empty board, and their
whole turn of development stops being able to fight Gatewatch at all. Then Tithe Collector
is a 3 mana 3/3 that wipes their 1-defence bodies on arrival, because the aura already took
them to one.

That pairing is the deck's best turn and it is entirely a sequencing problem. Hollowing then
Tithe Collector is a sweeper. Tithe Collector then Hollowing is a 3/3 and a ping.

The Assessor does the same job permanently and can be killed for it, which is the trade the
set keeps offering: the aura on a body never lapses but dies to removal, the aura on a rite
cannot be touched but runs out. Both in hand is the good spot. Both on the board stacks to
-2, and that is the turn you attack.

The Toll is face up from the moment it lands, and it is a genuine question rather than a
trap. The opponent is holding lethal-in-three-turns on your hero and reads *if you attack
my hero this turn, deal 2 damage to all your minions*. Going face costs them their board;
not going face costs them the clock. Lay it down while they still have a board worth
losing - against an empty board it taxes nothing.

Foreclosure is the punish for overcommitting and the most skill-testing card in the deck on
both sides. Armed on *enemy plays a minion* it takes the thing they just played, armed on
*enemy board reaches three* it takes whatever arrived last to get them there, and armed on
*enemy attacks hero* it takes the rightmost survivor of the swing. The opponent can play
around it by sequencing their good minion first and a cheap one after, so the card rewards
you for arming the trigger they are not watching for.

## Keeps

Keep Gatewatch, Coinbiter, Blood Rite. Keep Hollowing against a wide deck and throw it back
against a tall one. Throw back Pillarman, The Long Winter and Talent Scout always.
Foreclosure is a keep only on the draw.

## Tuning notes

- Every aura in the deck takes attack and never defence, and that rule should hold for the
  whole colour. A -1 defence board-wide aura kills every 1-defence minion the moment it lands,
  with no body to answer and no way to interact - that is unconditional removal wearing an
  aura costume, and it is the exact thing PHILOSOPHY.md keeps off the table.
- The Long Winter at -2 attack for three turns is the strongest card here and the first one
  to look at if the deck is winning too flatly. Against a board of 2/2s it is a sweeper that
  leaves the bodies standing and useless, which is more demoralising than killing them.
- Only one Knife Juggle. A second makes the deck a control deck, which is not what this is.
- Two Talent Scouts is the top end and the reason the deck is happy to be attacked: the
  Deathwish means killing the 5/5 hands you a minion. Against a deck that cannot attack
  around it, it is better than a 7 drop.
- Twelve distinct cards over 25 slots is the most singleton-ish list in the set. If the
  draws feel scattered, Pillarman is the cut - it is the only card with no interaction with
  the aura plan.

## Matchup: against Thornwake

Their auras add, yours subtract, and they cancel. Never open a window into a live one. The
patient line is to hold Hollowing until their Wildgrowth has ticked out, then drop it on the
turn their board is back to 1/2s and wipe it with Tithe Collector.

Their bodies are small and numerous, so The Toll is at its most expensive against them and
Knife Juggle at its best. Their Hold the Grove taxes you for casting - note that a wager and
an aura are both incantations now, so laying Hollowing down fires it. Sometimes that is just
the price.
